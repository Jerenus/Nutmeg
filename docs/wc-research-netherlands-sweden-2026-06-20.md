## 5. Tactical Matchup

- **Midfield:** Netherlands' De Jong–Reijnders–Gravenberch trio should control possession and tempo; Gravenberch was the MD1 standout (2 assists). Sweden's Potter 3-4-1-2 packs a midfield band (Karlström, Ayari, wide wing-backs Gudmundsson/Bernhardsson) but loses creative spine without Kulusevski — they're **functional through midfield, then direct to the strikers.** Edge: Netherlands on control.
- **Attacking threats both ways:** Real two-way threat. NED — Gakpo, Summerville, Malen + Dumfries overlaps; dangerous in transition and on the wings. SWE — the **Isak/Gyökeres duo is the most in-form strike pair in the tournament**; they punish any space and are excellent on the counter and at attacking crosses. This is the rare group game where the away side genuinely threatens.
- **Defensive vulnerabilities:** **Both defenses are soft.** NED conceded twice to Japan (late + set piece) with a makeshift Van Hecke–Van Dijk pairing (no De Ligt/De Vrij). Sweden have **zero clean sheets in 7 under Potter** and shipped one even while winning 5-1. Two leaky back lines + four high-quality forwards = goals likely at both ends.
- **Expected game state:** Sweden lead the group and have a cushion mindset but Potter's side plays open, not parked; Netherlands *need* a win and will press for it. Consensus = **entertaining, end-to-end, both teams score.**

**Goal expectation: HIGH-scoring / OVER 2.5.** Every preview and the goals market lean Over; ~10 combined goals in MD1, two vulnerable defenses, four elite strikers. This is a **3-goal-ish, BTTS game**, not a cagey low-scorer.

A 1-goal total would require both leaky defenses to shut down simultaneously, which contradicts every form/tactical signal here. Treat the engine's total=1 lean as the low-probability tail, not the base case.
